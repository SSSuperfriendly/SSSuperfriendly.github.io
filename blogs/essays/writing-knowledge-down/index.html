Write Knowledge Down

Aug 30, 2026 · 1 min read

Reading a book is easy, and so is retaining nothing of it afterwards. The hard part is closing the book and still being able to explain what you read in your own words. I often fall for the illusion that I “understood” a concept, only to find, flipping back through my notes, that all I kept were a few keywords with no real connection to what I already knew — those moments of “getting it” are usually just the fluency of reading flattering me, because reading smoothly is not the same as thinking clearly. So I made a habit: after learning something, close the source and write it down from memory. Whatever I cannot write, or can only write in fragments, is precisely what I have not yet understood. Writing is translating someone else’s logic into your own, and once the translation begins, the vague spots can no longer hide — every pause is understanding rewiring itself. Most of what is on this blog came from that habit: I write it down first, and only then slowly find out how much I truly know. Essays and notes are just forms; the plain sentence underneath never changes — write the knowledge down, and only then does it begin to belong to you.
